Grove Climber

The sprite a world model is being taught to imagine. It climbs four mossy ledges, collects a coin, and answers to six buttons. Every pixel is drawn procedurally — there are no image files anywhere in the project.

  • 1.71% of the frame
  • 6 actions
  • 128x128 at 15 fps
  • 2,025,385 frames recorded

Every state it can be seen in

Each action has to change the picture, or the model can predict the next frame while ignoring what you pressed. These eight views are the whole visual vocabulary.

Why it has a snout and a tail

The first version was a plain block with one eye. Facing left and facing right differed by eight pixels — the eye sliding across the head. That is a problem when left and right are the two most-used actions: the model could tell the character had moved, but not which way it was looking. The snout leads and the tail trails, so direction is now legible from the drawing itself. An earlier attempt shifted the whole sprite sideways to fake it; this one is a genuine side profile, mass pushed forward and tail trailing, so mirroring moves 71% of its pixels on its own.

The world it lives in

One episode start to finish: a four-hop climb to the top ledge. The route was not hand-designed. A search over 8,000 candidate layouts kept only those where simulated physics proved every platform reachable, and every launch window at least 24 pixels wide.

What a borrowed model saw instead

Before training anything, we fed our frames to a released world model trained on robotics video. Top row is ours; below is its reconstruction. It does not blur our pixel art — it replaces it, rebuilding the character as an articulated arm.

Seeded from one of our frames, its imagination continues somewhere else entirely: a walking figure on a checkered floor, sharp and coherent and completely wrong.

This is the failure the project exists to measure. A model entering territory its training never covered does not hesitate — it invents, confidently. Making that moment visible as a number that spikes while you watch is the goal.
